# Lesson 3 Plan — Deconstructing Counter-Arguments & Conceding Points

**Series:** KS3 English — Persuasive & Argumentative Writing  
**Topic Slug:** `ks3-disaster-committee`  
**Chapter Title:** Chapter 3: Dismantling Chief Engineer Vane's Objections  

**Curriculum Skills Taught:**
- Identifying counter-arguments and opponent objections in persuasive debate.
- Employing concessive transition structures ("While...", "Granted...", "Although...").
- Structuring counter-argument refutations (Concession → Pivot → Counter-Evidence).
- Recognizing logical fallacies in opposing claims (False Security & Downplaying Risk).

**Narrative Beat:** Chief Engineer Barnaby Vane storms into the Emergency Committee chamber! He claims the sea wall is structurally sound, accusing the committee of stirring public panic over a fake alarm. You must inspect Vane's flawed technical blueprints, acknowledge his valid point about municipal budget constraints, and dismantle his objection with irrefutable sensor data.

**Handoff Type:** Suspicion Climax  
**Handoff Text:** *"Chief Engineer Vane furiously rolls up his blueprints, but as he turns to leave, a heavy brass key drops from his coat pocket onto the council tiles—engraved with 'BUNKER 7: DOUGH-DOME ARCHIVE'. Before anyone can react, Vane snatches it up and locks himself inside the East Corridor."*

**Design Principle:** **Dynamic Component Diversity.** This lesson intentionally avoids repeating the component structures of Lessons 1 & 2. It introduces `annotateImage`, `multiSelectQuiz`, `matchingPairs`, `wordScramble`, `trueFalse`, and `quote` to keep the visual and interactive experience fresh.

### Slide 3: ⚖️ Scene 3: The Power of Concession

**Slide Purpose & Goal:** Teach the 3-step Counter-Argument Refutation Formula: 1) Concede (Acknowledge valid point), 2) Pivot (Use transition word), 3) Refute (Deliver killer counter-evidence).

**Components (in order of presentation):**

1. **`heading`**
   - **Props/Content outline:** `content: "⚖️ Scene 3: The Power of Concession"`
   - **Pedagogical purpose:** Concept title frame.
   - **Narrative function:** Unlocking the Refutation Blueprint.
   - **Placement rationale:** Top anchor.

2. **`callout`** (`variant: "important"`)
   - **Props/Content outline:** `"Officer's Handbook: Why Concede First?"` Acknowledging a valid part of your opponent's concern makes you sound fair, intelligent, and trustworthy. Once the audience trusts your fairness, your counter-evidence hits twice as hard!
   - **Pedagogical purpose:** Rule definition.
   - **Narrative function:** Strategic manual advice.
   - **Placement rationale:** Defines rationale before matching exercise.

3. **`matchingPairs`**
   - **Props/Content outline:** Match opposing claims to their strategic concessive pivots:
     - Pair 1: `Opponent: "Yellow Alert costs 50,000 pounds!"` ↔ `Concessive Pivot: "While municipal funds are limited, repairing flood damage will cost 2 million pounds."`
     - Pair 2: `Opponent: "The sea wall hasn't failed in 140 years!"` ↔ `Concessive Pivot: "Although the wall has a proud history, today's 1.8m surge is unprecedented."`
     - Pair 3: `Opponent: "Citizens might panic needlessly!"` ↔ `Concessive Pivot: "Granted, panic must be avoided, but clear emergency instructions create calm order."`
   - **Pedagogical purpose:** Applied matching connecting objections to concessive refutations.
   - **Narrative function:** Drafting rebuttals for Vane.
   - **Placement rationale:** Interactive application following concept callout.
   - **`markingMode` & rationale:** `auto-mark`.

**Components (in order of presentation):**

   - **Pedagogical purpose:** Scene title.
   - **Narrative function:** The formal debate in the council chamber.
   - **Placement rationale:** Top anchor.

2. **`paragraph`**
   - **Props/Content outline:** Vane steps up to the lectern: "You cannot prove that spending 50,000 pounds today saves anything!" You have 4 evidence statements prepared. You must select the 2 statements that combine a respectful concession with irrefutable proof.
   - **Pedagogical purpose:** Set up multi-select decision challenge.
   - **Narrative function:** Debate climax setup.
   - **Placement rationale:** Narrative context before choice.

3. **`multiSelectQuiz`**
   - **Props/Content outline:** Question: "Select the TWO strongest refutation options that combine a respectful concession with killer evidence against Engineer Vane:"
     - Option 1: "While 50,000 pounds is a significant council investment, independent sensors confirm sea water is already overtopping North Pier by 15 centimeters." (CORRECT)
     - Option 2: "Engineer Vane is completely wrong and knows nothing about sea walls." (INCORRECT - Insulting, no evidence)
     - Option 3: "Although the 1880 granite wall was built well, rusted drainage valves currently prevent any harbor water from receding." (CORRECT)
     - Option 4: "We should spend whatever money we want because safety is nice." (INCORRECT - Weak, vague)
     - Explanation: "Options 1 and 3 are powerful because both begin with a respectful concession ('While...', 'Although...') before delivering hard, irrefutable evidence!"
   - **Pedagogical purpose:** Evaluation of combined persuasive strategy.
   - **Narrative function:** Presenting your formal rebuttal speech.
   - **Placement rationale:** Central interactive decision component of Slide 4.
   - **`markingMode` & rationale:** `auto-mark`.

4. **`callout`** (`variant: "warning"`)
