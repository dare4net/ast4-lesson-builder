# Lesson 1 Plan — The Biscuit Tin Dilemma

**Series:** KS3 English — Persuasive & Argumentative Writing  
**Topic Slug:** `ks3-disaster-committee`  
**Chapter Title:** Chapter 1: The Biscuit Tin Dilemma  

**Curriculum Skills Taught:**
- Identifying and applying Aristotle's Triad of Persuasion (**Ethos, Pathos, Logos**) in written arguments.
- Distinguishing between authoritative appeal (ethos), emotional appeal (pathos), and logical evidence (logos).
- Constructing balanced 3-part persuasive appeals targeting specific audiences.

**Narrative Beat:** A Category 3 gale is battering Wittling-on-Sea, but the Emergency Committee has spent 3 hours debating font sizes on tea menus. You arrive as Junior Persuasion Officer. Using Ethos, Pathos, and Logos, you must convince Captain Bluster to declare an immediate Yellow Alert.

**Handoff Type:** Planted Suspicion  
**Handoff Text:** *"As Captain Bluster agrees to put the committee on yellow alert, a sudden shudder rattles the windowpanes. But the sound didn't come from the ocean outside. It came from deep underground, right beneath the town hall floorboards... and it smelled faintly of warm yeast and butter."*

**Design & Story Principles:**
- **Visual Character Introduction:** Slide 1 uses an interactive clickable character map (`hotspot`) and visual character dossiers so learners instantly meet the cast.
- **Purpose-Driven Composition:** Every component has explicit pedagogical purpose, narrative function, and placement rationale.
- **Zero HTML Tags:** Clean plain text throughout.
