# Lesson 2 Plan — The Art of the Opening Hook

**Series:** KS3 English — Persuasive & Argumentative Writing  
**Topic Slug:** `ks3-disaster-committee`  
**Chapter Title:** Chapter 2: The Art of the Opening Hook  

**Curriculum Skills Taught:**
- Writing high-impact opening hooks for persuasive speeches, notices, and articles.
- Employing 4 distinct hook engines: Rhetorical Question, Shocking Fact, Emotive Call to Action, and Dramatic Contrast.
- Structuring multi-sentence opening sequences (Hook → Context → Call to Action).

**Narrative Beat:** Town residents ignore dry, wordy official council notices pinned in Wittling town square. You teach the committee how to transform boring administrative announcements into irresistible digital billboard hooks on High Street.

**Handoff Type:** Discovery Object  
**Handoff Text:** *"As the new digital bulletin lights up the high street, forcing walking locals to pause in their tracks, Gerald Nibbs drops his leather briefcase. Out rolls a handwritten note on official Wittling Council letterhead: 'Project Dough-Dome: DO NOT LEAK TO PUBLIC.' Gerald quickly kicks it under a radiator before you can read the rest."*

**Design Principle:** **Dynamic Component Diversity.** This lesson intentionally avoids repeating the component structures of Lesson 1 and Lesson 3. It utilizes an interactive `flashcards` notice audit on Slide 1, `categorise` headline classification on Slide 2, and `annotateImage` notice breakdown on Slide 3. Zero HTML tags and zero filler.

### Slide 2: ⚡ Scene 2: The 4 Hook Engines

**Slide Purpose & Goal:** Classify 6 candidate headlines into the 4 primary Hook Engine categories (Rhetorical Question, Shocking Fact, Emotive Call, Dramatic Contrast) using an interactive `categorise` board.

**Components (in order of presentation):**

1. **`heading`**
   - **Props/Content outline:** `content: "⚡ Scene 2: The 4 Hook Engines"`
   - **Pedagogical purpose:** Concept title frame.
   - **Narrative function:** Unlocking the Officer's Hook Engine classification system.
   - **Placement rationale:** Top anchor.

2. **`paragraph`**
   - **Props/Content outline:** Professor Spindle opens the Officer's Field Manual: "A hook isn't random guesswork—it uses 4 distinct engine types to force a busy reader to freeze in their tracks."
   - **Pedagogical purpose:** Theory framing.
   - **Narrative function:** Briefing committee members on hook categories.
   - **Placement rationale:** Narrative context before sorting activity.

3. **`categorise`**
   - **Props/Content outline:** Categorize 4 headline items into 2 main Hook Engine categories:
     - Category 1 (`cat-q`): `Rhetorical Question`
     - Category 2 (`cat-f`): `Shocking Fact`
     - Item 1: `"Could your home survive a 6-foot wave?"` ➔ Category: `Rhetorical Question`
     - Item 2: `"Will Wittling be underwater before tea time?"` ➔ Category: `Rhetorical Question`
     - Item 3: `"Over 80% of our sea wall mortar has dissolved!"` ➔ Category: `Shocking Fact`
     - Item 4: `"Category 3 winds have breached North Pier by 1.5m!"` ➔ Category: `Shocking Fact`
   - **Pedagogical purpose:** Interactive sorting and pattern recognition of hook engines.
   - **Narrative function:** Sorting draft headlines in the committee office.
   - **Placement rationale:** Main interactive exercise of Slide 2.
   - **`markingMode` & rationale:** `auto-mark`.

### Slide 3: 🎯 Scene 3: Headline Transformation Workshop

**Slide Purpose & Goal:** Deconstruct Gerald's weak draft notice using an annotated diagram (`annotateImage`) and match dull announcements to high-impact hooks (`matchingPairs`).

**Components (in order of presentation):**

1. **`heading`**
   - **Props/Content outline:** `content: "🎯 Scene 3: Headline Transformation Workshop"`
   - **Pedagogical purpose:** Header anchor.
   - **Narrative function:** Critiquing Gerald Nibbs' draft billboard headline.
   - **Placement rationale:** Top anchor.

2. **`annotateImage`**
   - **Props/Content outline:** Draft council notice card with 3 concise visual annotation pins:
     - Pin 1 (Header): "Boring Header: 'Notice 4B'"
     - Pin 2 (Body): "Passive Words: 'Sea level variance'"
     - Pin 3 (Footer): "Zero Call to Action"
   - **AI Image Generation Prompt:** *"A close-up comic illustration of a crumpled white paper document titled 'Notice 4B: Regarding Sea Level Variance'. The paper has coffee stains, small faint blue pin markers labeled 1, 2, and 3 pointing at weak text sections, warm desk lighting, comic book graphic style."*
   - **Pedagogical purpose:** Visual inspection pointing out exact locations of weak writing.
   - **Narrative function:** Auditing Gerald's draft card.
   - **Placement rationale:** Interactive visual analysis feature of Slide 3.

3. **`matchingPairs`**
   - **Props/Content outline:** Match dull administrative sentences to transformed high-impact hooks:
     - Pair 1: `Dull: "Sea levels are increasing rapidly."` ↔ `Hook: "Is your doorstep ready for a 2-metre storm surge?"`
     - Pair 2: `Dull: "Citizens should pack belongings."` ↔ `Hook: "Pack your essentials now before the tide cuts off East Pier!"`
     - Pair 3: `Dull: "The weather is very severe today."` ↔ `Hook: "Category 3 winds are battering our coast right now!"`
   - **Pedagogical purpose:** Applied transformation exercise.
   - **Narrative function:** Rebuilding Gerald's notices into hooks.
   - **Placement rationale:** Follows annotated diagram for active transformation practice.
   - **`markingMode` & rationale:** `auto-mark`.
